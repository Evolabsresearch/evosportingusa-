# EVO Voice Standard

EVO Sporting USA should read like a real specialty strength store: direct, concrete, and interested in the gear before it talks about store mechanics.

## The Rule

Delete before adding. If a page already says shipping, returns, checkout, support, and policy links, do not restate them in a new card, badge, or paragraph.

## Page Jobs

Homepage: show the room, the gear, the categories, and the most useful starting points. Avoid policy recap blocks.

Collections: help people find the right station or training use. Use rack, bench, pull, dumbbell, conditioning, and recovery language.

Product pages: answer what ships, what it is for, materials, care, warranty, and related pieces.

Cart and checkout: show totals, shipping, line items, contact details, and secure payment fields. This is where transactional trust belongs.

Support and policy pages: answer service questions plainly. Do not reuse support language as homepage marketing copy.

## No-Slop Triggers

Rewrite copy that leans on:

- Repeated shipping or return claims outside transactional pages.
- "Before payment" on pages that are not checkout, billing, cart, or policies.
- Stat cards that exist only to prove the store has policies.
- Generic trust phrasing: visible path, support path, operating standard, customer terms, policy links stay visible, checkout terms.
- AI-ish contrasts like "not by impulse" or "cart that feels like a plan."
- Internal support labels on public pages: order record, support lookup, receipt path, fulfillment note, policy support, or faster first reply.
- Accounting/table labels on product or checkout pages: receipt notes, receipt email, statement name, card statement, warranty contact, support request, or support routing.
- Repeated grid badges that try to prove the store is real: Box contents, Warranty by item, Fast ship, Add-on favorite.
- Catalog copy that repeats the product name at the start of every sentence or feature. Use the name once, then talk about the belt width, wrap length, material, contents, or training station.
- Copy that tells the buyer the store is "ready" instead of telling them what the gear does, where it fits, or what to send when they need help.

## Better Patterns

Bad:

> Built like a store that has to answer customer questions. Product pages show what ships, what the piece is for, care notes, warranty coverage, and checkout terms before the order moves to payment.

Better:

> Equipment sorted by how it gets used. Start with the pull-day shelf, bench station, dumbbell lane, or recovery corner, then open the product page for contents, materials, care, and warranty.

Bad:

> Flat shipping is shown in the cart and checkout before payment details are entered.

Better:

> Cart and checkout can handle shipping details. The homepage should sell the gear.

## Repetition Budget

Homepage main content:

- shipping: 0 mentions
- returns: 0 mentions
- payment: 0 mentions
- checkout: 0 mentions unless linking directly to checkout
- support: only when used as a gear category, such as "lifting support"

Product page main content:

- shipping: 1 mention
- returns: 1 mention
- warranty: as needed for the product
- payment: avoid unless the component is checkout-related
- use shopper labels like "Best for," "Included," and "Materials" instead of database labels like "What ships," "Training role," or "Product specs"

Cart, checkout, policy, and support pages can use service terms when they directly answer the user's next question.
